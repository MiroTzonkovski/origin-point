Every result is the end of a chain.

What you get in life doesn't come out of nowhere. It comes from what you do, which comes from how you feel, which comes from what got wired into you long before you had a say in it.

  1. C

    Conditioning Your origin point

    What got wired in before you could choose: your beliefs, your emotions, what you learned you're worth.

  2. S

    State

    How you feel in the moment. Set by that wiring, not by what's actually happening.

  3. B

    Behavior

    What you do from that state.

  4. R

    Result

    What you get. Every time.

Most people start at the bottom. They fix the behavior: new habits, new routines, a new plan, a new city.

That's the same person in a different outfit.

The result doesn't change until you change where the chain starts.

Where I found mine

From the outside, everything was working. My results kept growing. I was doing the right things.

How I felt didn't follow. Some days I felt unstoppable. Other days I felt like I wasn't worth a penny.

Same person. Same results. Same plan.

So my state wasn't coming from my results. It was coming from further back: beliefs I'd picked up, emotions I'd never looked at, a sense of my own worth that was wired in long before any of it.

That's the start of the chain. It's where I went, and it's where The Origin Point starts with you.

What The Origin Point is

1-1 coaching that starts at the root, not the result. Not new habits. Not motivation. You find the question your whole life has been answering, and you rewrite it.

You can't see the filter you're looking through. That's why doing this alone stalls.

That's what working 1-1 is for. It happens in two parts.

  1. 1

    The preparation

    You learn how your mind builds what you experience: your model of the world, cause and effect, why perception is projection. This is what makes everything after it hold.

  2. 2

    The intervention

    You go to the start of your chain, beginning with your drive: the question running underneath every decision you make. You find the old one, see what it has cost you, and design a new one.
